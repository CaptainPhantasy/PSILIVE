MATERIAL CONTEXT

General construction-era context (not a defect statement)

Cast iron was the prevailing material for residential laterals in Indianapolis through the mid-twentieth century. Cast-iron lateral pipe has a typical service life of approximately 50 to 75 years, after which interior corrosion — pitting, scaling, and tuberculation — is commonly observed. Central Indiana soils are clay-heavy and undergo seasonal expansion and contraction; over time, this soil movement can contribute to joint separation, belly formation, and stress at fittings.

The findings that follow should be read against that backdrop. None of this context, on its own, is a defect statement; it is included so this report can be interpreted in the same frame of reference the engineering team used when reviewing the footage.

LIMITATIONS & EXCLUSIONS

What this inspection does not cover

 

The end of the lateral run, including any connection to a municipal main or easement-side cleanout downstream of the terminal point reached, was not reached and was not inspected.

Pipe material was identified visually through the camera. No laboratory testing or destructive material verification was performed.

Slope and grade measurement was not available with the camera equipment used. Bellies are reported by visual evidence of standing fluid and grade variation, not by surveyed slope.

This inspection covers the visible and accessible portions of the lateral sewer line only. Branch lines not accessed by the camera, the municipal main, and any portion of the system not entered by the pushrod are outside the scope of this report.
