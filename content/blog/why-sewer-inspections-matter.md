## Your Sewer Line Is the Most Expensive Thing You Can't See

When most people think about home maintenance, they think about roofs, HVAC systems, and appliances. But there's a critical piece of infrastructure running beneath your property that rarely gets attention until something goes catastrophically wrong: your sewer line.

Your main sewer line connects your home to the city sewer system. It carries every drop of wastewater from your sinks, toilets, showers, and washing machine. When it fails, the consequences range from inconvenient backups to devastating property damage costing $10,000 to $25,000 or more in repairs.

## The Cost of Ignoring Your Sewer Line

Sewer line problems don't announce themselves with obvious warning signs — at least not until they've become serious. Root intrusion, for example, can slowly strangle your pipe over years before you notice slower drains. By the time sewage backs up into your basement, the damage is done.

Here's what makes sewer problems so expensive: the pipe runs underground, often beneath your yard, driveway, or even your foundation. Repairing or replacing it means excavation, which means heavy equipment, landscaping destruction, and potentially weeks of disruption.

## When Should You Get a Sewer Inspection?

**Buying a home:** This is the most critical time. Standard home inspections don't include sewer scopes, and sellers aren't required to disclose problems they don't know about. A pre-purchase sewer inspection gives you negotiating power and protects you from inheriting someone else's problem.

**Owning a home with older pipes:** If your home was built before 1980, there's a good chance you have clay or cast iron pipes that are approaching or past their expected lifespan. Regular inspections help you plan for maintenance before emergencies happen.

**Experiencing warning signs:** Slow drains, gurgling sounds, sewage odors, or wet spots in your yard are all signs that something may be wrong underground.

**Before major landscaping or construction:** If you're planning to dig near your sewer line, knowing exactly where it runs and what condition it's in can prevent accidental damage.
