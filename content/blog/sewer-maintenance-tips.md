## Prevention Is Always Cheaper Than Repair

Your sewer line is one of the most expensive components of your home's infrastructure to repair or replace. The good news is that most sewer problems are preventable with basic awareness and maintenance. Here are seven tips that can save you thousands in emergency repairs.

## 1. Know What Not to Flush

Your toilet is designed for human waste and toilet paper — nothing else. These items cause the majority of preventable sewer blockages:

- **"Flushable" wipes** — Despite the marketing, these don't break down like toilet paper and are the leading cause of sewer blockages nationwide
- **Feminine hygiene products** — These expand in water and create stubborn clogs
- **Paper towels and tissues** — Much thicker than toilet paper and don't dissolve
- **Cotton swabs, dental floss, and hair** — These tangle together and catch on pipe imperfections

A simple rule: if it's not human waste or toilet paper, it goes in the trash.

## 2. Be Smart About Grease

Cooking grease is the silent killer of sewer lines. When you pour hot grease down the drain, it cools and solidifies inside your pipes, gradually building up until it creates a complete blockage.

Instead, let grease cool in the pan, then wipe it into the trash. For larger amounts, pour it into a disposable container and throw it away. This single habit can prevent the most common kitchen-related sewer problems.

## 3. Be Mindful of Trees and Landscaping

Tree roots are the number one cause of sewer line damage in Central Indiana. Roots are naturally attracted to the moisture and nutrients inside sewer pipes, and they can travel surprising distances to reach them.

Before planting trees or large shrubs, know where your sewer line runs. Avoid planting water-seeking species like willows, maples, and poplars near the line. If you already have trees near your sewer line, regular inspections can catch root intrusion early.

## 5. Watch for Warning Signs

Your sewer line often gives subtle warnings before a major failure. Pay attention to:

- **Multiple slow drains** — If more than one drain is slow, the problem is likely in the main sewer line, not individual fixtures
- **Gurgling sounds** — Air bubbles in your drains indicate a partial blockage downstream
- **Sewage odors** — Sewer gas escaping through drains or from your yard suggests a break or blockage
- **Wet spots in your yard** — Unexplained soggy areas above your sewer line may indicate a leak
- **Foundation cracks** — In severe cases, sewer line problems can affect your foundation

## 6. Know When to Call a Professional

DIY drain cleaning with store-bought chemicals can actually damage your pipes, especially older clay or cast iron lines. Chemical drain cleaners are corrosive and can accelerate pipe deterioration.

If you're experiencing recurring drainage issues, call a professional. A sewer scope inspection will identify the actual cause so the right solution can be applied — whether that's a simple cleaning, root treatment, or something more involved.

## 7. Document Your Sewer Line's Condition

Having a baseline video inspection of your sewer line is invaluable. It gives you a reference point to compare against future inspections, helps you track the progression of any identified issues, and provides documentation for insurance claims or real estate transactions.

Keep your inspection videos and reports in a safe place. They're part of your home's maintenance history.
